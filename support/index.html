Help for Pars Color Lab on iPhone and iPad. Start with the answers below, or contact the developer directly.

How do I change my learning level?

Open Settings and select Beginner, Intermediate or Expert. The lesson text and quizzes change with your level. You can also take the optional assessment in Learn; its suggestion never changes your level automatically.

Why does a mixed color look different in real paint?

The app uses demonstration pigment spectra. Lighting, screen settings, pigment brand, binder and film thickness affect real results. Use the app to explore; validate a physical sample before mixing a production batch.

How do photos and camera access work?

Use Color Match to select a photo or take one, then tap a region to sample its color. Camera permission is optional. If denied, enable it in Settings → Apps → Pars Color Lab → Camera. Photo processing takes place on your device.

How do I protect or move saved work?

In Settings, use Export backup to create a JSON file of saved colors, formulas and estimates. Save it somewhere you control. Import backup adds records; importing the same file more than once can create duplicates. Lesson progress is not included in this backup.

How do I back up Studio data?

Personal paints, Industrial Lab measurements and mix-correction comparisons are Studio data. They have their own backup in Settings → Studio data & device transfer, separate from the main backup above. Move the file with Files or AirDrop and restore it on the other device; a restore replaces the current Studio records. Lesson progress is in neither backup.

What does Photo Studio do?

Open Photo Studio from Home, choose a photo, take one or try the built-in example. It extracts a 3–10 color palette, shows a value histogram, a grayscale value study, a simplified version and an optional 3×3, 6×6 or 9×9 drawing grid. Export the palette as CSV or share the study image as PNG, including the grid when it is on. Analysis runs on the device and the app does not keep your photo.

Where do the artist paint references come from?

The paint library contains 7,971 community RGB screen references across 24 brands, from an open-source MIT-licensed dataset. They are approximate on-screen colors, not manufacturer-measured spectral data, and metallic or fluorescent paints are not reproduced. Add your own paints or import them from a CSV file.

How do I import Industrial Lab CSV files?

In Formulas → Industrial Lab, open CSV templates to see the expected columns, or load the labeled demonstration data. Measurements must use D65 illuminant and 2° observer with matching geometry. Spectral files need 31 bands, R400 to R700 in 10 nm steps, with explicit fraction or percent units. There is no direct instrument connection: export from your instrument software and map the columns to the template.

What does Pars Color Lab Pro include?

Mixing, target matching, Color Match, formulas, coverage, all lessons, the glossary, the pigment library and backups are free. Pro adds Photo Studio, the artist paint references, artist tools, Industrial Lab, mix correction and PDF formula reports. It costs $1.99 per month or $19.99 per year (US prices; the app shows your local price). Cancel at least 24 hours before renewal in Settings → your name → Subscriptions. On a new device, open any Pro tool and tap Restore purchases.

How do I cancel Pro?

Cancel anytime. On your iPhone or iPad open Settings → your name → Subscriptions → Pars Color Lab, or tap Cancel or manage subscription on the Pro screen in the app. Cancel at least 24 hours before renewal; Pro stays active until the end of the paid period. Apple handles all cancellations and refunds.

Does the app require an account or internet connection?

No account is required. Mixing, calculations, lessons and saved work run locally. Opening an external lesson source, sharing a file or contacting support may use other apps and an internet connection.

How do I delete my data?

Delete individual saved items in the app. Deleting the app through the Settings app removes its local app data; offloading the app retains data. Copies you exported or system backups must be managed separately.

Color simulations and demonstration pigment data are educational estimates. Results are not manufacturer-calibrated paint recipes. Test real materials before practical use.
